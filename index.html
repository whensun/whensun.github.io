Computer Science PhD Student

Theodorus Wensan Februanto

I work on computer systems and security, with a focus on RISC-V, system software, specification-guided testing, and LLM-assisted analysis.

I am a PhD student in Computer Science at Arizona State University. My research studies how complex low-level systems can be tested and validated automatically from specifications and system documentation.

I am especially interested in operating systems, hypervisors, firmware, trusted execution environments, and architectural behavior.

I am advised by Prof. Adil Ahmad at the ASTeRiSC Research Lab.

01

Research Interests

RISC-V Systems

Understanding and testing architectural behavior across processors, emulators, operating systems, hypervisors, and firmware.

Specification-Guided Testing

Translating natural language specifications into machine-checkable rules and executable test cases.

Systems Security

Studying isolation, privilege boundaries, context switching, and security-sensitive architectural state.

LLM-assisted Systems Analysis

Using large language models to understand specifications, source code, and system documentation for automated testing and security analysis.

Firmware and Hardware Interfaces

Analyzing low-level firmware behavior, device registers, memory-mapped I/O, and hardware-software interaction.

Program Analysis

Exploring program analysis techniques for low-level software, including firmware and kernel-level systems.

Research Highlights

44

RISC-V ISA Conformance Bugs

Discovered 44 previously unknown ISA conformance bugs across multiple RISC-V emulators, with 40 confirmed or fixed upstream.

13

Context-Switch Isolation Bugs

Preliminary results identified 13 bugs across 74 evaluated registers in OpenSBI, Linux/KVM, Penglai, and ACE-RISC-V. Findings have been reported to the respective maintainers.

PoC

Security Validation

Developing attack PoCs for exposed architectural state, including performance counters such as time in U-mode and VS-mode.
